To the Editor, International Journal of Astrobiology:

We submit our Research Article, "The Wow! Signal: Exploring the Boundaries
of Evidence and Inference," for consideration.

The paper conducts a bounded forensic reassessment of the surviving evidence
for the 1977 Wow! signal across five published hypotheses. Its central
result is an identifiability boundary: the surviving record supports
quantitative hypothesis-specific constraints (a brightness-temperature bound,
a verified stochastic-repeater likelihood, candidate-specific comet
exclusion) but not a unified five-way Bayesian posterior.

Disclosure on key sources: the frozen observational vector (flux, angular
size, duration, frequency) is taken from Mendez et al. 2025 (arXiv:2508.10657),
and the tested H3 transient-flare hypothesis from Mendez, Ortiz Ceballos &
Zuluaga 2024 (arXiv:2408.08513) — both unrefereed preprints at the time of
writing (reportedly submitted to ApJ). This dependence is stated explicitly
in the manuscript (Secs. 3.1 and 6.1), and the affected sections are
identified there.
